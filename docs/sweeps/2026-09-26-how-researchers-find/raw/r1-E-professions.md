## Findings

F1. **Hypothesis-first, then break it apart.** Investigative journalists write the story as a hypothesis of three sentences or fewer. They split it into its terms, and each term produces the next questions to answer. If the hypothesis turns out false, that is treated as a lead, not a loss.
    who: Mark Lee Hunter (INSEAD adjunct, journalist), Hanson, Sabbagh, Sengers, Sullivan, Svith, Thordsen. Published free by UNESCO in 2011. The authors also train people in this method.
    evidence: "it's also great if the statement is not true, because that means there may be a better story than the one you originally imagined." — http://scoop.mk/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Manual-Mark-Lee-Hunter.pdf (2026-09-26; READ-PARTIAL, ch. 2, 3, 5, 7)
    bears on: Q4, Q5, C1, C5
    limit: The same manual warns: "Try to disprove them as well as prove them."

F2. **Use open records the competition ignores, then ask people to confirm rather than tell.**
    who: same manual (Hunter, ch. 3)
    evidence: "Never forget: It is always easier to get someone to confirm something you already know or have understood, than to get them to volunteer information you do not possess." / "Do not assume that because it is open to the public, this information is old, worthless, already known." — same PDF (READ-PARTIAL)
    bears on: Q3, C4
    limit: The manual says open sources can be thin in some countries.

F3. **One chronological "master file" where connections surface as you type, plus a source map for routing around people who won't talk.**
    who: Hunter; Flemming Svith (DICAR)
    evidence: "As you create the master file, connections between different data points, as well as events or facts that seem to make no clear sense, will become evident to you." / "if one source is blocked, you can go to another source who can see past the obstacle." — same PDF, ch. 3 and 5
    bears on: Q4, Q7, C3
    limit: Instructions for one person or a small team. Security rule: keep source identities out of the file.

F4. **Pivot chain when a search runs dry: an assumption, then asking people, then records, then an absence noticed, then leaked data, then sources that don't depend on each other.** Reverse image search found nothing. The team reasoned that the men were likely GRU officers and asked former officers which academy would have trained them. They searched yearbook photos from that academy, then combined three facts in one query ("DVOKU", "Chechnya", "Hero of Russia"), which surfaced the name. From there they used leaked phone and residence databases from 2003 and 2012, then a passport file.
    who: Bellingcat and The Insider investigation team. Bellingcat is a nonprofit that also sells workshops.
    evidence: "Based on the array of information sources consulted – all of which were independent from each other and came from different time periods – Bellingcat was able to conclude with certainty" — https://www.bellingcat.com/news/uk-and-europe/2018/09/26/skripal-suspect-boshirov-identified-gru-colonel-anatoliy-chepiga/ (2026-09-26; READ-FULL)
    bears on: Q3, Q4, C1, C3
    limit: The rare records came from leaked databases and passport files obtained from sources, which raises legal and ethical questions.

F5. **A dead end becomes a pivot through a shared intermediary.** Nominee directors lead back to the lawyers and company-formation agents behind them, and criminals reuse the same ones. Searching the intermediary's name can open dozens of related companies. Link-analysis software then flags entities that repeat across documents.
    who: Paul Radu, co-founder of OCCRP. Handbook published by ICFJ.
    evidence: "Searches performed for names of such companies often lead to lawyers or designated shareholders. But this should not be seen as a dead end." / "If the same address is encountered on page 564 as it is on page 3, I2 will find this connection" — https://web.archive.org/web/20200706122418if_/http://test.irrp.org.ua/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/Follow-The-Money.pdf (2026-09-26; READ-PARTIAL)
    bears on: Q3, Q4, C3
    limit: Radu says fieldwork is "equally important". In his refinery case, the link came from neighbours and a licence plate.

F6. **Short sellers go for the whole obscure dataset and compare filings across countries.** Hindenburg downloaded an entire offshore corporate registry. Muddy Waters compared a company's US SEC filings with its Chinese regulatory filings, and also read physical clues on a site visit.
    who: Nate Anderson / Hindenburg (a short seller that disclosed its short position, so it talks its book; wound down Jan 2025). Carson Block / Muddy Waters, as told in Richard Teitelbaum's book (a secondary source quoting Block).
    evidence: "Our research, which included downloading and cataloguing the entire Mauritius corporate registry, has uncovered that Vinod Adani… manages a vast labyrinth of offshore shell entities." — https://hindenburgresearch.com/adani/ (READ-PARTIAL). "we believe the most impactful research results from uncovering hard-to-find information from atypical sources" — https://hindenburgresearch.com/about-us/ (READ-FULL). "Sean noticed that the road didn't have the kind of wear and tear you would expect." — https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsvb32s953xerr4gnshs/portfolio/book-excerpt-muddy-waters-carson-block-on-short-selling (READ-FULL, secondary). All fetched 2026-09-26.
    bears on: Q3, C4
    limit: Both firms profited from their conclusions.

F7. **Scuttlebutt: ask a cross-section of people around a company about each other; go to management last, only to fill gaps.**
    who: Philip Fisher, money manager, *Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits* (1958)
    evidence: "Go to five companies in an industry, ask each of them intelligent questions about the strength and weakness of the other four, and nine times out of ten a surprisingly detailed and accurate picture of all five will emerge." — https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Philip_Arthur_Fisher (ROUTER; 1997 ed. p.17. Buffettpedia's wording differs slightly. Primary BLOCKED.)
    bears on: Q3, Q5, C4
    limit: Relies on people talking when they don't expect to be quoted. It predates expert-network insider-trading law.

F8. **Fraud examiners argue both sides on purpose and loop through a hypothesis until it is proven, disproven, or unprovable.**
    who: ACFE, *Fraud Examiners Manual* 2022 (the ACFE sells the certification that teaches this)
    evidence: "To prove that a fraud has occurred, the fraud examiner must seek to prove that fraud has not occurred. The reverse is also true." — https://www.acfe.com/-/media/images/acfe/products/publication/fraud-examiners-manual/2022_fem_sample_chapter.ashx (2026-09-26; READ-PARTIAL)
    bears on: Q5, Q6, C5, C1
    limit: The hypothesis is set to the "worst-case scenario". The legal standard of proof drives the both-sides rule.

F9. **Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH).** Lay all evidence against all hypotheses, look for what would disprove them, and ask what should be visible if a hypothesis were true but isn't. Evidence that fits every hypothesis is worth nothing.
    who: US Government Tradecraft Primer (2009); Richards Heuer (CIA, 45 years)
    evidence: "Ask what evidence is not being seen but would be expected for a given hypothesis to be true." — https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Tradecraft-Primer-apr09.pdf (READ-FULL). "Unproven hypotheses should be kept alive until they can be disproved." — https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Pyschology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.pdf (READ-PARTIAL, ch. 5 and 8). Both fetched 2026-09-26.
    bears on: Q2, Q5, C5
    limit: The primer says ACH is "most effective with a small team".

F10. **Quality-of-information check: a database of sources, and a periodic re-read of evidence you already dismissed.**
    who: Tradecraft Primer
    evidence: "Having multiple sources on an issue is not a substitute for having good information that has been thoroughly examined." Also: "Reexamine previously dismissed information in light of new facts." — Primer (READ-FULL)
    bears on: Q2, Q4, C4
    limit: Its warning is that judgments become "anchored to weak information", with the old caveats forgotten.

F11. **An audit of where a real review's sources came from: citation-chasing dominated, the planned search did not.** Of 495 sources, 30% came from the planned database and hand searches, 51% from snowballing (references of references), and 24% from personal knowledge or contacts. Database searching yielded about one useful paper per 40 minutes, hand searching one per 9 hours, and forward citation tracking one per 15 minutes. The work began with "extensive browsing".
    who: Trisha Greenhalgh and Richard Peacock (UCL), auditing their own review
    evidence: "Fifty one per cent were identified by "snowballing" (such as pursuing references of references), and 24% by personal knowledge or personal contacts." — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1283190/ (2026-09-26; READ-FULL)
    bears on: Q1, Q3, Q4, C1, C2
    limit: Applies to "complex evidence". For clinical trials, four databases suffice (see A5).

F12. **Where systematic reviewers find unpublished work.** Cochrane's routes: write to trial authors with the list of studies found so far, search trial registers and regulators' clinical study reports, and use Freedom of Information requests. Checking reference lists is mandatory.
    who: Lefebvre, Glanville et al., Cochrane Information Retrieval Methods Group (information specialists)
    evidence: "send a comprehensive list of relevant articles and study records along with the eligibility criteria for the review to the first author… asking if they know of any additional studies" — https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-04 (2026-09-26; READ-FULL)
    bears on: Q3, Q7
    limit: The chapter itself says "Asking researchers… has not always been found to be fruitful."

F13. **Patent examiners revise the search as results come in and chain citations in both directions.**
    who: European Patent Office, Guidelines B-IV 2.2 and 2.4 (2025 edition)
    evidence: "the search process is interactive and iterative in the sense that the search division will revise its initial search statements depending on how useful it considers the information it has found so far" — https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2025/b_iv_2_2.html. Section 2.4 adds documents "cited in" and "citing" a relevant find: https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2025/b_iv_2_4.html (both 2026-09-26; READ-FULL)
    bears on: Q4, C1
    limit: A revised edition is now in force; I read the 2025 text.

F14. **Search by what a thing does, not what it is called; browse classification codes where words fail.**
    who: USPTO, *Manual of Patent Examining Procedure* §904
    evidence: "a tea mixer and a concrete mixer may both be regarded as relating to the mixing art, this being the necessary function of each." / "it is rare that a text search alone will constitute a thorough search" — https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s904.html (2026-09-26; READ-FULL)
    bears on: Q3
    limit: Fast-moving fields need non-patent literature because patents "seriously lag behind invention".

F15. **ICIJ's "radical sharing": findings and query tips posted as the work happens, to topic groups, on a shared graph of entities.** The Global I-Hub has groups by country or subject. Graph views of findings can be saved and passed to colleagues abroad.
    who: Emilia Díaz-Struck, Cécile Schilis-Gallego, Pierre Romera (ICIJ staff describing their own system)
    evidence: "ICIJ's partners communicate their findings as they are working, not only with their immediate co-workers, but also with journalists halfway around the world." / "sharing not only findings and results of the reporting, but also useful tips on query strategies." — https://datajournalism.com/read/handbook/two/working-with-data/how-icij-deals-with-huge-data-dumps-like-the-panama-and-paradise-papers (2026-09-26; READ-FULL)
    bears on: Q7, C6, C3
    limit: ICIJ says it "took several projects to get reporters comfortable". Mandatory security (PGP, 2FA, client certificates) gates the sharing. The chapter has no explicit rule against doing work twice.

F16. **Stopping rules.**
    who: EPO; Cochrane
    evidence: "end its search once the probability of discovering any more relevant prior art becomes very low in comparison to the work involved." — https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2025/b_iv_2_6.html. Cochrane: stop "if adding in a series of new terms… yields no new relevant records". It warns that only finding the known key papers "might be a sign that the strategy is biased towards known studies". It also names capture-recapture and relative recall. — Cochrane ch. 4.4.11 (READ-FULL)
    bears on: Q6
    limit: Cochrane: "there has been little formal evaluation of the approaches."

## Against the claims

A1. **Against C3: Heuer on the "mosaic" picture.** Analysts form the picture first and then pick the pieces. Collection should be aimed narrowly at telling hypotheses apart.
    who: Heuer, *Psychology of Intelligence Analysis*, ch. 5
    evidence: "Accurate estimates depend at least as much upon the mental model used in forming the picture as upon the number of pieces of the puzzle that have been collected." — CIA PDF (READ-PARTIAL)
    bears on: C3, C1, Q4
    limit: In finance, the "mosaic theory" is a sanctioned practice (see Frontier).

A2. **Against C1 (unlimited accretion) and C5 ("become sure").** Past a minimum amount of information, more information does not improve accuracy, but it does raise confidence.
    who: Heuer, ch. 5 (citing Slovic's horse-racing handicapper experiment)
    evidence: "Once an experienced analyst has the minimum information necessary to make an informed judgment, obtaining additional information generally does not improve the accuracy of his or her estimates." — CIA PDF (READ-PARTIAL)
    bears on: C1, C5, Q6
    limit: Heuer says it should "not necessarily be accepted at face value".

A3. **Against C2: patent examiners search narrow first and widen only if needed.**
    who: EPO B-IV 2.2; USPTO MPEP §904
    evidence: "It will always consider the results already obtained in deciding whether it needs to widen the search to include other, less relevant parts of the documentation." — EPO 2.2 (READ-FULL). MPEP: "the most likely areas of finding relevant prior art are searched first."
    bears on: C2
    limit: The target is well defined (the claims). Journalism's hypothesis-first method (F1) also starts narrow.

A4. **Against C6: duplication is deliberate for judgment and deliberately avoided for search, and one intelligence technique keeps teams apart on purpose.** Cochrane does not run searches in parallel; it peer-reviews the search plan before running it. It does require two people to screen studies independently. Team A/Team B separates analysts so the disagreement stays visible.
    who: Cochrane ch. 4; Tradecraft Primer
    evidence: "it is not considered necessary (or even desirable) for two people to conduct independent searches in parallel." — Cochrane 4.2.2. "Use (at least) two people working independently to determine whether each study meets the eligibility criteria" — Cochrane MECIR C39 (READ-FULL). The Primer's Team A/Team B keeps "competing and possibly equally strong mind-sets" separate.
    bears on: C6, Q7
    limit: none stated

A5. **Against C4 as a universal rule: in well-indexed fields, the rare sources rarely change the answer.**
    who: Cochrane ch. 4 (citing Hartling 2017); Greenhalgh 2005 (citing Royle & Milne)
    evidence: "unpublished studies and studies in languages other than English rarely had any impact on the results and conclusions of the review." — Cochrane 4.3.2. "Searching 26 further databases identified only an extra 2.4% of trials." — Greenhalgh (READ-FULL)
    bears on: C4, Q3, Q6
    limit: The exceptions are "few relevant studies" or "questionable vested interests". Complex evidence behaves differently (F11).

## Not covered by any claim

N1. **Absence as a lead.** A missing trace points to the next search.
    evidence: "The systematic omission from photographs of an otherwise notable figure… suggested that Colonel Chepiga may also be a secret service officer." — Bellingcat (READ-FULL). Heuer: "If deception is planned well… one should not expect to find evidence of it readily at hand."
    bears on: Q3, Q5

N2. **Order of approach: from the edge inward.** Start with neutral outsiders, then the less-involved, and reach the main target last. Fraud examiners, Fisher (management last) and journalists (confirm, don't ask) independently converge on this.
    evidence: "fraud examiners should start interviewing at the periphery of all possible interview candidates and move toward the witnesses who appear more involved" — ACFE (READ-PARTIAL)
    bears on: Q3, Q4

N3. **Peer-review the search plan before running it, and log how each find was reached.**
    evidence: Cochrane: "all search strategies should be peer reviewed, before being run" (the PRESS checklist). Berkeley Protocol: "record the pathways to relevant material, including the terms, operators and the search engines that led to that content." — https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/2024-01/OHCHR_BerkeleyProtocol.pdf (READ-PARTIAL)
    bears on: Q2, Q7

N4. **Build the query list for both sides and in every relevant language.**
    evidence: Keywords "in all relevant languages and scripts"; "identify what might qualify as incriminating and exonerating information" — Berkeley Protocol ¶141(c)
    bears on: Q3, Q5, C5

N5. **Every step needs a stated reason (predication).**
    evidence: "If fraud examiners cannot articulate a factual basis or good reason for an investigative step, they should not do it." — ACFE (READ-PARTIAL)
    bears on: Q4, Q6

N6. **A central research desk with a ticket queue, instead of peers passing leads to each other.**
    evidence: OCCRP ID works with "dozens of publicly available, but little-known, open sources" and "a bespoke ticketing system to shorten the lead time from story inception to publication" — https://id.occrp.org/about/ (READ-FULL)
    bears on: Q7, Q3

N7. **Publish the method so outsiders can check it and add to it.**
    evidence: "our work can be reverse engineered. If there are any flaws in our methodology, they're readily apparent. However, this transparency also enables others to contribute additional information." — Eliot Higgins, https://thoughteconomics.com/eliot-higgins/ (READ-FULL)
    bears on: Q7, Q5
